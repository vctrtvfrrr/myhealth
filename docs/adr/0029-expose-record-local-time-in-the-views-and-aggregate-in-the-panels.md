# Expose Record Local Time in the views and aggregate in the panels

A dashboard cuts days, weeks and months by Record Local Time, the wall-clock time a Health Record happened at under its own offset, so a trip does not move a daily total. The views of `read_model` hold the instant in UTC and the offset as `±HH:MM` text, and the local time follows from adding the offset as an interval, an expression easy to get wrong because `at time zone` reads a numeric offset with the POSIX sign.

The views therefore expose Record Local Time, computed once by the migrations and covered by tests: every instant with an offset gains a `<name>_local` column of type `timestamp`, appended at the end of its view, and every child view (sleep sessions and stages, binned series, route points) joins its parent and exposes its own instants in local time under the parent's offset. Snapshot Windows are already local and stay as they are. A record whose source gave no offset was stored as `+00:00` and falls on its UTC day; nothing tells it apart from a record made in UTC, and that limitation is accepted.

The views do not aggregate. Totals, minimums, maximums and the goal beside them are written in each panel's SQL, because the granularity is a dashboard variable ("Agrupar por": hour, day, week, month) and a view takes no parameter: aggregating in views would mean one view per metric per granularity, each a migration only the dashboards read. One immutable function, `read_model.local_bucket(granularity, local_time)`, gives the start of the bucket in local time, with weeks starting on Sunday as the phone's calendar does; it lives in the read model so the Sunday rule is written and tested in one place, and PostgreSQL grants `EXECUTE` to `PUBLIC` by default, so the read account needs no new grant. With a coarser granularity than a day, the goal line is the sum of the daily goals in force on each day of the bucket; with hours there is no goal line.

Grafana reads a `timestamp` without time zone as UTC, and its time range reaches the SQL as UTC instants. A dashboard in UTC would show the wall clock unchanged, but then "Today" is the UTC day, already tomorrow at 21:00 in São Paulo, and "now" sits hours ahead. The dashboards keep the viewer's time zone instead and move the wall clock into it: the `time` column is `local_bucket(...) at time zone '$__timezone'`, an instant whose clock in the viewer's zone reads the record's wall clock, and the range filter compares the local column with `$__timeFrom()::timestamptz at time zone '$__timezone'` and its `$__timeTo()` counterpart. `$__timeGroup` is not used, since it only has fixed buckets aligned to the UTC epoch. The conversion stays in the panel rather than in the function, so the function knows nothing of Grafana.

Because the aggregation lives in the panels, their SQL is verified the way ADR 0028 runs every panel, through a real Grafana for every value of "Agrupar por", and the local day and the goal in force are among the rules it asserts exactly. `$__timezone`, like "Agrupar por", is a dashboard variable the browser interpolates, not a macro the datasource expands, so whatever calls `/api/ds/query` substitutes it, with `America/Sao_Paulo`.
