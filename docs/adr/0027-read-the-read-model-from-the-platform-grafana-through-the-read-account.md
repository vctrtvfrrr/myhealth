# Read the read model from the platform Grafana through the read account

The Personal Health History is to be seen in the Grafana of the CodeLab platform, and a stack cannot hand that Grafana a datasource. The platform provisions datasources itself, from `codelab/infra`, and today only `prometheus` and `loki`; its reconciler (ADR-0008 of `codelab/infra`) rejects a whole observability generation that queries any other datasource, and a rejected generation keeps the API from being deployed; and the Grafana is not on the `postgres` network. Publishing the history as Prometheus metrics was rejected because the metrics endpoint must not carry personal data and a scrape cannot hold a history. A Grafana of the stack's own was rejected because it would need its own route, Cloudflare Access application, login, storage and upgrades, duplicating what the platform already puts around its single operator.

The platform changes instead. Its Grafana joins the `postgres` network and connects to `postgres:5432`, as every consumer of the shared PostgreSQL does; the platform provisions a PostgreSQL datasource with the fixed UID `myhealth-read`, not editable in the UI, on the `myhealth` database; and ADR-0008 of `codelab/infra` is amended so that the reconciler accepts that UID only in the generations of the `myhealth` application, never in another stack's. The platform change ships first: a generation naming `myhealth-read` before the reconciler accepts it is rejected, and the API stops deploying.

The datasource logs in as `myhealth_read`, the account the Data Owner already reads with, rather than an account of its own. Grafana therefore reads exactly what ADR 0013 grants, the views of `read_model` and nothing of the ingestion tables, including through the free SQL its Explore offers, and the grants and the step that restores them after a Full Purge stay as they are. The cost is the password: it now also lives in a key of the platform's Ansible Vault, rendered into the datasource's secure fields, besides the Data Owner's private store, and rotating it means altering the role, updating both and re-running the Grafana playbook, which breaks the dashboards and the Data Owner's `psql` access together until both are updated.

What the dashboards show must therefore be a view in `read_model`: data held only in the ingestion tables reaches Grafana by becoming a view, or not at all. The empty generation of ADR 0005 stands until the first dashboard is published.
